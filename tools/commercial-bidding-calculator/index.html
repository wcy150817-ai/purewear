Bidding on commercial cleaning contracts can be daunting. Bid too high, and you lose the job. Bid too low, and you win the job but lose money. This guide and calculator will help you pinpoint the exact right price for any commercial facility.

How to Bid Commercial Cleaning Contracts

Bidding isn't just about throwing out a number. It requires a systematic approach to estimating labor, understanding your overhead, and protecting your profit margins. Here are the core steps to accurately bidding any commercial space.

1. The Walk-Through is Mandatory

Never bid a commercial contract without seeing the facility. During your walk-through, you need to assess:

  • Total cleanable square footage: Don't just take the building's total size. Subtract areas you won't be cleaning (like locked server rooms or specific storage areas).
  • Floor types: Carpet vacuums faster than hard floors need to be mopped and polished.
  • Density and Obstacles: An open warehouse is much faster to clean than a densely packed call center with hundreds of cubicles.
  • Restroom count: Restrooms are the most time-consuming part of any facility. Count the fixtures (toilets, urinals, sinks).

2. Calculate Production Rates (Cleaning Speed)

Your production rate is how many square feet one cleaner can clean in one hour. Industry averages for standard office spaces are usually around 2,500 to 3,500 sq ft per hour. However, this drops significantly for medical facilities (1,500 sq ft/hr) due to higher sanitation standards, or increases for industrial spaces (4,000+ sq ft/hr).

3. Determine Labor Costs

Once you know how long it will take, multiply those hours by your fully-loaded labor rate. This shouldn't just be the hourly wage (e.g., $15/hr). It must include payroll taxes, workers' compensation insurance, and benefits. Your "loaded" rate might actually be $18-$20/hr.

4. Add Overhead and Supplies

Overhead includes your insurance, vehicles, office expenses, marketing, and cleaning supplies. Many cleaning companies calculate this as a percentage of labor costs. Typically, supplies and overhead add another 15% to 25% to your base costs.

5. Apply Your Target Profit Margin

Finally, add your profit margin. Healthy commercial cleaning businesses aim for a net profit margin of 15% to 25%. If your total costs (labor + overhead) are $100 and you want a 20% margin, your selling price is not $120. It's $100 / (1 - 0.20) = $125.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a commercial cleaning bid?

To calculate a commercial cleaning bid, you need to determine the square footage of the facility, estimate the time required to clean it based on production rates (e.g., 2,500 sq ft per hour), multiply the hours by your hourly labor cost, and then add your overhead, supplies cost, and desired profit margin.

What is a good profit margin for commercial cleaning?

A typical net profit margin for a commercial cleaning business ranges from 10% to 28%. It's important to accurately track all labor and overhead costs to ensure your target margin is actually achieved.

How much should I charge per square foot?

While charging per square foot is common, the rate varies widely depending on the facility type, frequency of cleaning, and geographic location. Rates typically range from $0.05 to $0.20 per square foot for standard office cleaning.
