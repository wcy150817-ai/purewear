Every week, thousands of commercial cleaning contractors search how to calculate cleaning cost per square foot when preparing a Request for Proposal (RFP) for a corporate office, medical clinic, school, or HOA clubhouse. Yet relying on a flat "rule-of-thumb" square-footage price—such as quoting $0.12 per square foot blindly across every building—is the single fastest way to go broke in the janitorial industry.

A 25,000-square-foot distribution warehouse with two single-stall restrooms and wide concrete aisles can be cleaned in 5.5 hours at $0.05 per square foot and generate a 32% net profit margin. A 25,000-square-foot urgent-care medical clinic with 28 exam rooms, 28 sinks, and terminal hospital-grade disinfection dwell times requires 16.5 hours per night—meaning a $0.10/sq ft bid would lose money on every single shift before you even buy trash liners.

Professional commercial estimators never start with a price per square foot. Instead, they calculate labor hours from ISSA 612 Production Rates & Cleaning Units, apply a Fully Burdened Hourly Wage (including FICA, unemployment, and Workers' Comp), add consumable supply and overhead percentages, divide by (1 − Target Gross Margin), and then derive the resulting price per square foot as a final sanity check. 1. The 5-Step Commercial Janitorial Bidding Formula

Whether you are bidding a 3-day-per-week dental office or a 5-night-per-week Class-A corporate headquarters, every profitable commercial janitorial contract follows five sequential equations:

📐 COMMERCIAL JANITORIAL BIDDING & GROSS MARGIN DIVISOR EQUATIONS
1. Monthly Visits (V_mo) = Days_Per_Week × 4.333 weeks/month
2. Monthly Labor Hours (H_mo) = (Cleanable_SqFt ÷ ISSA_Production_Rate) × V_mo
3. Fully Burdened Hourly Cost (W_burdened) = Base_Hourly_Wage × (1 + Payroll_Burden_%)
4. Total Monthly Direct + Overhead Cost (C_total) = (H_mo × W_burdened) + Consumable_Supplies + Admin_Overhead
5. Monthly Contract Bid Price = C_total ÷ (1 − Target_Gross_Margin_%)
6. Effective Monthly Price Per Sq Ft = Monthly Contract Bid Price ÷ Cleanable_SqFt
Why 4.333 Weeks Per Month Is Mandatory:
• A calendar year contains 52 weeks ÷ 12 months = 4.3333 weeks per month. If you multiply a 5-day-per-week contract by 4.0 weeks (20 visits) instead of 4.333 weeks (21.67 visits), you give away 20 free cleanings per year—almost an entire month of unpaid labor!

3. Decoding Payroll Burden: Why a $17.50/hr Cleaner Actually Costs $21.70/hr

The number-one accounting mistake made by first-year janitorial bidders is multiplying monthly labor hours by the cleaner's raw W-2 base wage. When you hire an employee at $17.50 per hour, federal and state laws require you to pay an additional 20% to 32% in statutory payroll burden on every hour worked:

  • Employer FICA Taxes (7.65% Mandatory): Under the Federal Insurance Contributions Act, employers pay 6.20% toward Social Security and 1.45% toward Medicare on every dollar of gross wages.
  • Federal & State Unemployment Insurance (FUTA + SUTA: ~1.5% to 4.5%): FUTA is effectively 0.6% on the first $7,000 of wages per employee, while SUTA ranges from 1.0% to 4.0%+ depending on your state and experience rating.
  • Janitorial Workers' Compensation (NCCI Class Code 9014: ~4.5% to 9.5%): Because commercial custodians lift mop buckets, operate rotary buffers, and handle diluted disinfectants, NCCI Class Code 9014 carries a higher premium rate than clerical staff.
  • General Liability Insurance, Dishonesty Bonding & PTO (~3.0% to 6.0%): Commercial property managers typically require a $1M/$2M General Liability certificate plus a Janitorial Services Bond, alongside uniform allowances and state-mandated paid sick leave.

Combined, a realistic commercial janitorial payroll burden is 24.0%. On a $17.50/hr base wage, your true direct labor floor is $17.50 × 1.24 = $21.70 per hour. 4. Gross Margin Divisor vs Markup Percentage: Avoiding the 5% Profit Leak

Suppose your total monthly direct labor, payroll burden, chemical supplies, and administrative overhead for a corporate office contract equal exactly $3,000.00 per month, and your company targets a 25% gross profit margin:

  • The Wrong Way (25% Markup): Multiplying $3,000 × 1.25 = $3,750.00 gives you $750 in gross profit. However, $750 ÷ $3,750 = 20.0% gross margin—you silently lost 5 full percentage points of margin!
  • The Right Way (Gross Margin Divisor): Dividing $3,000 ÷ (1 − 0.25) = $3,000 ÷ 0.75 = $4,000.00 gives you $1,000 in gross profit, which is exactly $1,000 ÷ $4,000 = 25.0% gross margin.

6. Frequently Asked Questions (Commercial Janitorial Bidding)

How do you calculate commercial cleaning cost per square foot?

Professional janitorial estimators calculate cost per square foot from the bottom up rather than guessing a flat rate. First, divide cleanable square footage by the facility's ISSA production rate (sq ft/hr) to find labor hours per visit, then multiply by monthly visits (Days/Week × 4.333) and your fully burdened hourly wage (Base Wage × [1 + Payroll Burden %]). Add monthly consumable supplies and overhead, divide by (1 − Target Gross Margin %), and finally divide the resulting monthly contract price by cleanable square footage to see your exact effective price per square foot.

What is the average commercial cleaning price per square foot in 2026?

In 2026, standard commercial office cleaning averages $0.08 to $0.20 per cleanable square foot per visit, or $0.11 to $0.32 per square foot per month for 5-day-per-week service (21.67 visits/month). Medical and dental clinics average $0.13 to $0.29 per sq ft per visit ($0.28 to $0.54/sq ft monthly) due to hospital-grade disinfectant dwell times, while open industrial warehouses average $0.04 to $0.08 per sq ft per visit.

What is payroll burden in a commercial janitorial bid?

Payroll burden is the mandatory employer cost added on top of a cleaner's base hourly W-2 wage. In the commercial janitorial industry, payroll burden typically ranges from 20% to 32% of gross wages and includes employer FICA taxes (7.65% for Social Security and Medicare), federal and state unemployment insurance (FUTA/SUTA, 1.5% to 4.0%), janitorial Workers' Compensation insurance (NCCI Class Code 9014, typically 4.5% to 9.5%), general liability insurance, and paid time off.

Why should janitorial bids divide by (1 - Gross Margin) instead of multiplying by (1 + Markup)?

Multiplying your total monthly cost by 1.25 applies a 25% markup, which only yields a 20.0% gross profit margin ($250 profit on a $1,250 bid). Dividing your total monthly cost by (1 − 0.25 = 0.75) yields the true gross margin price ($1,333.33 bid on $1,000 cost, leaving $333.33 or exactly 25.0% of top-line revenue as gross profit).
