How to Maintain a 30% Net Profit Margin in Cleaning

Many cleaning business owners fall into the trap of measuring business health purely by gross revenue. However, after accounting for field labor wages, chemical supplies, microfiber replacement, vehicle fuel, and general liability insurance, healthy cleaning services target a 25% to 35% net profit margin.

The Net Profit Margin Formula

📐 CLEANING SERVICE MARGIN & CASH FLOW FORMULAS
\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Total Client Billings} - \text{Direct Route Labor}
\text{Net Operating Income (NOI)} = \text{Gross Profit} - (\text{Chemicals} + \text{Fuel} + \text{Insurance} + \text{Software})
\text{Net Profit Margin (\%)} = \frac{\text{Net Operating Income}}{\text{Total Revenue}} \times 100
Custodial Accounting Targets:
• Direct Labor Target: 40% – 45% (highest single expenditure item).
• Supply Ratio: 5% – 8% (slashed below 5% using 1:32 / 1:64 bulk chemical dilution).
• Target Net Margin: 25% – 35% ensures business longevity through economic downturns and seasonal lulls.

💡 Practical Case Study: Lifting Monthly Net Margin From 12% to 28%
1
Diagnose Expense Leaks: A residential cleaning business generated $12,000/mo but retained only $1,440 (12% margin) due to non-clustered cross-town driving and retail cleaning supply purchases.
2
Route Re-Clustering: Grouped service appointments into 3 compact zip-code clusters, cutting weekly vehicle gas and maintenance overhead by $340/month.
3
Bulk Chemical Dilution: Switched from $5.50 retail spray bottles to bulk concentrates, reducing supply expense from 8.2% to 4.1% ($490/month saved).
4
Financial Result: Monthly take-home profit surged to $3,360 (28% net margin) without needing to raise customer prices.
