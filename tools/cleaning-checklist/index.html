Standard House Cleaning Checklist (Room by Room)

This is the written version of the default generator output. It describes a standard recurring clean: surfaces that people touch and see are cleaned every visit, while inside-appliance and detail work is left for deep cleans. Adjust it to the home, but resist the urge to remove items just because they are rarely dirty; a checklist only works if it is the same every time.

Kitchen

  • Clear, wipe and sanitize countertops and the backsplash; move small appliances to wipe underneath.
  • Scrub the sink basin and polish the faucet and handles; run the disposal with water if there is one.
  • Wipe the fronts of the refrigerator, oven, dishwasher and microwave, including handles.
  • Clean the microwave inside and out.
  • Degrease the stovetop, burners or glass top, and the control knobs.
  • Spot-wipe cabinet doors and handles, focusing on the ones near the stove and trash.
  • Empty the trash, wipe the lid, and replace the liner.
  • Vacuum or sweep, then damp-mop the floor, finishing at the doorway.

Bathrooms

  • Apply cleaner or disinfectant to the toilet, sink and tub/shower first so it can sit while you work elsewhere in the room.
  • Clean the toilet bowl, seat (both sides), hinge area, tank and base.
  • Scrub shower walls, glass doors and the tub; remove soap scum and hard-water spots.
  • Clean mirrors and chrome fixtures streak-free.
  • Wipe the vanity counter, sink and cabinet fronts.
  • Wipe light switches, towel bars and door handles.
  • Empty the wastebasket and replace the liner.
  • Mop the floor, paying attention to the area around the toilet base.

Bedrooms

  • Make the bed or change linens if a fresh set has been left out.
  • Dust nightstands, dressers, headboard, lamps and picture frames.
  • Clean mirrors and any glass surfaces.
  • Empty trash bins.
  • Vacuum carpet, including edges and under the bed as far as reachable; mop hard floors.

Living and dining areas

  • Dust shelves, the TV stand (not the screen with a wet cloth), side tables and décor.
  • Wipe the dining table and chairs.
  • Vacuum upholstered furniture and under cushions; straighten pillows and throws.
  • Dust reachable ceiling fan blades and light fixtures.
  • Clean glass tabletops and fingerprints on sliding doors.
  • Vacuum rugs and floors; mop hard floors last.

Commercial Office Cleaning Checklist

Office cleaning is usually sold as a nightly or few-times-a-week service with a separate list of periodic tasks. The "Commercial Office" and "Commercial Restroom" zones in the generator follow this split.

Every service

  • Empty trash and recycling at desks and common areas; replace liners.
  • Wipe and sanitize break-room counters, sink, tables and appliance handles.
  • Clean and disinfect restrooms: toilets, urinals, sinks, counters, mirrors, partitions touch points.
  • Restock soap, paper towels, toilet paper and seat covers.
  • Wipe high-touch points: door handles, push plates, light switches, elevator buttons, shared equipment.
  • Vacuum walkways and carpeted traffic lanes; sweep and damp-mop hard floors and the entrance.

Weekly or periodic

  • Dust desks that have been cleared, partitions, window sills and ledges.
  • Vacuum edges, under desks and behind doors.
  • Clean interior glass, entry doors and sidelights.
  • Clean the inside of the break-room microwave and refrigerator (agree in writing who removes food).
  • Detail restroom grout and floor drains; high dust vents and light fixtures.

Restroom and Disinfection Steps That Checklists Often Miss

Wiping a surface with disinfectant and drying it straight away is the most common reason a "disinfected" restroom is not. The U.S. EPA's guidance on safe and effective disinfectant use lists the steps: use a product with an EPA registration number for the job, read and follow the label directions, pre-clean visibly dirty surfaces, keep the surface wet for the full contact (dwell) time stated on the label, wear the protective equipment the label calls for and wash your hands afterwards, and store the product closed and out of reach. Contact times differ by product, so the checklist should say "per label" rather than a fixed number of minutes.

Two related points belong on any commercial checklist. First, mix concentrates at the label ratio; the disinfectant ounces-per-gallon calculator converts 1:64, 1:128 and 1:256 ratios into spray-bottle and bucket amounts. Second, under OSHA's Hazard Communication Standard (29 CFR 1910.1200), a portable container filled from a labeled container only skips labeling if it is for the immediate use of the employee who filled it; a spray bottle that is stored, shared, or left on a cart needs a workplace label. The OSHA secondary container label generator prints one, and the chemical safety checker flags combinations that should never be mixed.

The Order of Work: Top to Bottom, Dry to Wet, Clean to Dirty

The generator lists tasks in the order most professional cleaners work, because order is what keeps a clean fast. Three rules cover almost everything:

  • Top to bottom. Dust falls. Do ceiling fans, shelves and counters before floors, so the vacuum picks up whatever came down.
  • Dry to wet. Dust and vacuum before you wipe or mop. Wet cloths turn loose dust into streaks.
  • Clean to dirty, and use dwell time. Spray bathroom fixtures first and let the product sit while you clean mirrors and counters; finish with the toilet and then the floor, working back towards the door. Keep separate cloths (many crews colour-code them) for toilets so they never touch kitchen surfaces.

How to Score a Cleaning Inspection

📐 INSPECTION SCORE
\text{Inspection Score (\%)} = \frac{\text{Tasks Passed}}{\text{Tasks Inspected}} \times 100
Illustrative example: a supervisor checks 31 tasks after a standard four-room clean and finds 2 missed (inside microwave and bedroom baseboard). Score = 29 ÷ 31 × 100 = 93.5%. The pass mark is your own business decision; what matters is using the same list each time so scores are comparable between cleaners and visits.

Track which tasks fail most often, not just the total. If "inside microwave" fails on several jobs, the fix is training or moving it earlier in the order of work, not a longer checklist. Scores like this also make performance reviews fairer; the cleaning staff performance review generator can use them as evidence.

Common Checklist Mistakes

  • Vague tasks. "Clean kitchen" cannot be inspected. "Wipe appliance fronts and handles" can.
  • Lists that change every visit. Rotating detail tasks is fine; dropping basics is not.
  • No scope boundary. State what is not included (inside oven, exterior windows, laundry) so the client is not surprised.
  • Ignoring the label. A checklist that says "disinfect" without "leave wet for label contact time" invites shortcuts.
  • No record. A printed or copied completion report is your evidence if a client disputes a visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should be on a house cleaning checklist?
A standard house cleaning checklist covers the kitchen (counters, sink, appliance fronts, microwave, stovetop, floor), bathrooms (toilet, tub or shower, sink, mirrors, floor), bedrooms (beds, dusting, mirrors, vacuuming) and living areas (dusting, upholstery, floors). Deep and move-out cleans add inside appliances, cabinets, baseboards, vents and window tracks.
What is the difference between a standard clean and a deep clean?
A standard clean maintains surfaces that are used and seen every week. A deep clean adds detail work such as the inside of the oven and refrigerator, baseboards, door frames, vent covers, window sills and tracks, and grout. Many services do a deep clean first and then switch to standard recurring visits.
What order should you clean a room in?
Work top to bottom, dry to wet, and clean to dirty: dust high surfaces first, then counters, then floors; dust and vacuum before wet wiping or mopping; and in bathrooms apply product to fixtures first so it can sit for its label contact time, finishing with the toilet and the floor.
Can I print this cleaning checklist?
Yes. Choose the rooms in the Checklist Manager and click Print Sheet. The page prints in black and white with the navigation and sidebar hidden, sized for standard US Letter paper. You can also copy a plain-text completion report to paste into a message or your scheduling software.
What should an office cleaning checklist include?
Every service: trash and recycling, break-room surfaces, restroom cleaning and disinfection, restocking paper and soap, high-touch points such as door handles and switches, and vacuuming and mopping traffic areas. Periodically: dusting cleared desks and ledges, edge vacuuming, interior glass, inside break-room appliances, and high dusting.
