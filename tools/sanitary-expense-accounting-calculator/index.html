1. The 5 Core Janitorial Supply & Gross Margin Accounting Equations

To diagnose supply leakage on your P&L, separate your sanitary expenditures into three distinct cost pools—Direct Chemical & Tool COGS, Unbilled Restroom Consumables Leakage, and Equipment Depreciation / Safe Harbor Expensing:

📐 JANITORIAL SANITARY EXPENSE RATIO, MARKUP & PER-VISIT SURCHARGE FORMULAS
1. Core Chemical & Microfiber Ratio (%) = [(Monthly_Chemicals + Microfiber_PPE_Mops) ÷ Gross_Monthly_Revenue] × 100 (Target: 1.8% – 3.5%)
2. Total Sanitary & Equipment COGS (%) = [(Chemicals + Microfiber_PPE + Absorbed_Paper_Liners + Equipment_Amort) ÷ Gross_Monthly_Revenue] × 100
3. Billable Restroom Supply Resale Revenue = Client_Paper_Liner_Wholesale_Cost ÷ (1 - Target_Supply_Margin_%) (e.g., Cost ÷ 0.70 = +42.8% markup)
4. Monthly Sanitary Overrun ($) = max(0, Actual_Sanitary_Spend - [Gross_Monthly_Revenue × Target_Ratio_0.03])
5. Per-Job Supply Recovery Surcharge = Monthly_Sanitary_Overrun ÷ Monthly_Completed_Jobs_Or_Visits

3. How to Price Restroom Consumables (Divisor Margin Math vs. Markup)

When a commercial facility manager asks you to supply their restroom paper products, high-density trash can liners, and dispenser soap, never add a simple 15% markup—and never bundle those consumables inside a flat monthly labor fee. Use the Gross Margin Divisor Formula:

  • Target 30% Gross Margin on Consumables: If a case of 1,000-ft hardwound roll paper towels costs you $42.00 wholesale from your janitorial distributor, divide by (1 - 0.30) = 0.70: $42.00 ÷ 0.70 = $60.00 client invoice price (a 42.86% markup that yields a true 30.0% gross margin, or $18.00 gross profit per case to cover warehouse storage, delivery, and restocking labor).
  • Residential Maid Service "Green / Eco Supply Surcharge" ($5 – $12 Per Visit): If your residential cleaning company’s chemical, trash bag, vacuum HEPA bag, and microfiber laundry expense creeps above 3.0% of revenue, adding a transparent $6.00 per-visit Environmental & Sanitization Supply Fee across 250 monthly house cleans generates $1,500/month ($18,000/year)—100% offsetting your entire annual chemical and vacuum replacement budget!

4. IRS Schedule C & Corporate Tax Deductions: Line 22 vs. $2,500 De Minimis Safe Harbor

Cleaning business owners frequently overpay federal and self-employment taxes by misclassifying equipment and supplies on their tax return:

  1. IRS Schedule C Line 22 (Supplies): Deduct all consumable cleaning supplies that are used up within 12 months—including Quaternary Ammonium and hydrogen peroxide concentrates, toilet bowl cleaners, melamine erasers, microfiber cloths, mop heads, trigger sprayers, and nitrile gloves.
  2. Treas. Reg. § 1.263(a)-1(f) De Minimis Safe Harbor Election ($2,500 Per Item Rule): Normally, commercial equipment lasting more than 1 year must be capitalized and depreciated. However, by attaching the annual Section 1.263(a)-1(f) De Minimis Safe Harbor Election statement to your tax return, you can immediately expense 100% of any commercial backpack vacuum ($650), orbital floor machine ($1,400), carpet spotter ($950), or pure-water DI tank ($1,850) that costs $2,500 or less per invoice item.
  3. IRC Section 179 for Heavy Machinery (>$2,500): Ride-on battery auto-scrubbers ($9,500), propane burnishers ($4,200), and truck-mounted steam extractors ($24,000) exceeding the $2,500 threshold can still be deducted 100% in Year 1 using IRC Section 179 expensing.

6. Frequently Asked Questions (Janitorial Chart of Accounts & Sanitary Expense Ratios)

What should a cleaning company's sanitary supply and chemical expense percentage of revenue be in 2026?

For a well-run residential maid service or commercial janitorial contractor using metered chemical concentrates, core cleaning chemicals, microfiber towels, nitrile gloves, and small tools should represent only 1.8% to 3.5% of gross revenue. If your sanitary supply COGS exceeds 4.0% (excluding billable client restroom paper and trash liners), your crews are either free-pouring chemical concentrates, buying retail ready-to-use spray bottles instead of 1:128/1:256 concentrates, or absorbing client restroom consumables without billing them back.

Are janitorial cleaning supplies classified as Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) or Operating Overhead on tax returns?

In managerial P&L accounting (QuickBooks / Xero), janitorial companies divide supplies into two Cost of Service / COGS accounts: Account 5210 (Direct Cleaning Chemicals, Microfiber & PPE consumed on jobs) and Account 5220 (Billable Client Restroom Consumables — toilet tissue, multi-fold paper towels, trash can liners, and foaming hand soap resold to commercial buildings). On IRS Schedule C (Form 1040), sole proprietors deduct routine cleaning supplies on Line 22 ("Supplies") and small equipment under $2,500 per item under the IRC Section 1.263(a)-1(f) De Minimis Safe Harbor election.

Why should commercial janitorial contractors never include free toilet paper and trash liners in a flat-rate cleaning contract?

When a commercial building increases employee headcount or visitor foot traffic, restroom paper towel, jumbo bath tissue, foaming soap, and high-density can liner consumption can jump by 40% to 80% overnight—completely wiping out your janitorial contract's net profit margin. Always separate "Restroom Consumable Restocking" from your base labor contract and bill paper/liners on a separate monthly invoice with a 25% to 35% gross margin.

How do you write off commercial floor scrubbers, backpack vacuums, and water-fed poles in 2026?

Under the IRS De Minimis Safe Harbor (Treas. Reg. § 1.263(a)-1(f)), any individual vacuum, extractor, rotary floor machine, or water-fed pole costing $2,500 or less per invoice item ($5,000 with an Applicable Financial Statement) can be expensed 100% in the year purchased rather than depreciated over 5 years. Larger ride-on auto-scrubbers or commercial truckmounts exceeding $2,500 qualify for immediate write-off under IRC Section 179.
