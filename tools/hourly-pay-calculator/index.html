The FLSA Overtime Pay Formula

📐 FLSA STATUTORY OVERTIME & PAYCHECK FORMULAS
\text{Regular Pay} = \min(\text{Hours}, 40) \times \text{Base Hourly Rate}
\text{Overtime Pay} = \max(0, \text{Hours} - 40) \times (\text{Base Hourly Rate} \times M_{\text{OT}})
\text{Gross Weekly Pay} = \text{Regular Pay} + \text{Overtime Pay}
\text{Net Take-Home Pay} = \text{Gross Pay} \times (1 - \text{Tax Rate}_{\text{effective}})
FLSA Section 7(a) Overtime Rules:
• Non-Exempt Threshold: Custodial workers, housekeepers, and janitors are non-exempt employees entitled to mandatory 1.5× overtime.
• Bi-Weekly Averaging Prohibited: Employers cannot average hours across two weeks (e.g. 50 hours in week 1 and 30 hours in week 2 cannot be averaged as 40 + 40 to avoid 10 hours of overtime).
• Discretionary vs Non-Discretionary Bonuses: Attendance or production bonuses must be factored into the regular rate when computing overtime.

Understanding Gross Pay vs. Net Take-Home Pay

Gross pay represents the entire sum earned before any payroll deductions. In contrast, net take-home pay is the actual amount received via direct deposit. For W-2 employees, typical mandatory deductions include:

  • Federal Income Tax: 10% to 22% for most service workers.
  • FICA (Social Security & Medicare): 7.65% fixed employee share (6.2% Social Security + 1.45% Medicare).
  • State & Local Income Tax: 0% to 8% depending on state residence.
💡 Practical Case Study: Calculating a 45-Hour Week for a Lead Cleaner
1
Determine Regular Wages: 40 regular hours at $22.00/hr = \(40 \times \$22.00 = \$880.00\).
2
Calculate Overtime Premium: 5 overtime hours at \(1.5 \times \$22.00 = \$33.00/\text{hr}\), yielding \(5 \times \$33.00 = \$165.00\).
3
Total Gross Earnings: \(\$880.00 + \$165.00 = \mathbf{\$1,045.00}\) for the week (effective average rate of $23.22/hr).
4
Net Take-Home Estimation: With an 18% effective withholding rate (FICA + federal tax), take-home pay is \(\$1,045.00 \times 0.82 = \mathbf{\$856.90}\).

Frequently Asked Questions

How is 1.5x overtime calculated?
Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), any hours worked over 40 in a single 7-day workweek must be compensated at a minimum of 1.5 times the employee's regular hourly rate. For example, if your base pay is $20.00/hr, your overtime rate is $30.00/hr ($20 × 1.5).
What is double-time pay (2.0x)?
Double-time is twice the regular hourly rate ($40.00/hr on a $20.00 base). While federal law does not mandate double-time, certain states (like California for shifts exceeding 12 hours in a single day) or union collective bargaining agreements require it for holiday or weekend work.
How do independent 1099 cleaners handle taxes?
Solo cleaners operating as independent 1099 contractors must pay self-employment tax (15.3% for both employer and employee FICA shares) plus income taxes. They should set aside 25% to 30% of every dollar earned into a dedicated tax account.
