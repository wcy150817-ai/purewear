The 4-Step Time Prediction Blueprint

ISSA Weighted Labor Formula:
Base Labor Hours = 1.2 hrs (Kitchen/Entry baseline) + (Sq Ft ÷ 1,400) + (Bedrooms × 0.25h) + (Bathrooms × 0.50h)
Total Labor Hours = (Base Labor Hours × Service Type Multiplier × Condition Multiplier) + Add-ons
On-Site Duration = Total Labor Hours ÷ Number of Crew Cleaners

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How long does it take one cleaner to clean a 2,000 sq ft house?
For a standard recurring maintenance cleaning (dusting, vacuuming, mopping, kitchen, and 2.5 bathrooms), one solo cleaner typically requires 3.5 to 4.5 hours (approx. 450-550 sq ft per hour). If it is a first-time deep clean or move-out clean, it requires 6.0 to 8.0 man-hours.
How does crew size affect total on-site cleaning time?
Total man-hours remain roughly constant, but on-site duration scales inversely with crew size. For example, a 6 man-hour job takes 6 hours for a solo cleaner, 3 hours for a 2-person crew, and 2 hours for a 3-person crew. Note that teams larger than 3 may experience slight coordination drag in smaller homes.
Why does a move-out or deep clean take nearly double standard time?
Deep cleanings and move-out turns involve high-labor tasks omitted from routine visits: hand-scrubbing soap scum from shower grout, cleaning inside kitchen appliances (oven, fridge), detailing baseboards, door frames, window tracks, and inside kitchen cabinets.
What is the recommended buffer time between cleaning appointments?
Professional cleaning services recommend adding a 30 to 45-minute buffer between appointments. This accounts for packing/unpacking equipment, equipment sanitization, traffic transit, and minor on-site client walkthrough delays.
